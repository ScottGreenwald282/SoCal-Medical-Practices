DSCR stands for Debt Service Coverage Ratio &mdash; and it&#39;s the single most useful loan in real estate investing that most banks never mention. Here&#39;s the entire concept in one sentence: the property qualifies, not you. A DSCR lender looks at one core question: does the property&#39;s rental income cover the mortgage payment? If the rent covers the payment (or comes close), the deal can work &mdash; regardless of what your personal tax returns say. No W2s. No pay stubs. No personal debt-to-income calculation. Your write-offs, your other mortgages, your self-employment income structure &mdash; none of it gets in the way. That&#39;s why investors use DSCR loans to scale portfolios. A conventional loan starts fighting you around property #4 or #5 (your DTI fills up). DSCR loans don&#39;t care how many properties you own &mdash; each deal stands on its own numbers. How the DSCR Ratio Actually Works The math is simple: Monthly Rent &divide; Monthly Payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, HOA) = DSCR. DSCR of 1.0 = rent exactly covers the payment DSCR above 1.0 = property cash flows (lenders love this &mdash; better rates) DSCR below 1.0 = rent doesn&#39;t fully cover &mdash; and here&#39;s what most investors don&#39;t know: plenty of lenders still do these deals. Sub-1.0 DSCR programs exist; they just want more down or price the rate accordingly. Here&#39;s what banks don&#39;t tell you: DSCR requirements vary wildly by lender. One lender wants 1.25. Another takes 1.0. Another does 0.75 with the right down payment. This is exactly why working with a broker matters &mdash; I shop your deal across dozens of investor-focused lenders and match your property&#39;s numbers to the lender who&#39;ll actually say yes. What You Need to Qualify for a DSCR Loan in Arizona Typical DSCR loan requirements (they vary by lender &mdash; that&#39;s the point of shopping it): Down payment: usually 20-25% for a purchase (80% max LTV is common; some go higher) Credit score: most programs start around 620-660; better scores unlock better pricing The property&#39;s rent: documented by a lease or the appraiser&#39;s market-rent analysis (Form 1007) &mdash; meaning yes, you can use PROJECTED rent on a property you haven&#39;t rented yet Reserves: commonly a few months of payments in the bank Entity-friendly: most DSCR lenders let you close in an LLC &mdash; a big deal for investors who don&#39;t want personal-name title What you DON&#39;T need: tax returns, W2s, pay stubs, employment verification, or a personal DTI that survives underwriting.
