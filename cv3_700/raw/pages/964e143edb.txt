What Is a DSCR Loan?
What is a DSCR loan? A DSCR loan is an investment property loan that qualifies borrowers based on rental income instead of personal income. Gross monthly rent divided by monthly PITIA equals the DSCR ratio. A ratio of 1.0 means breakeven. Above 1.0 is positive cash flow. Baltimore investors in well-positioned neighborhoods regularly achieve 1.20 to 1.45.

Why Baltimore Is a Strong Market for DSCR Investors
The Hopkins employment anchor is the foundation. Medical residents, researchers, hospital administrators, and university faculty create a professional renter base that cycles through neighborhoods like Charles Village, Remington, and Waverly with low vacancy and consistent payment history. Beyond Hopkins, the University of Maryland Medical Center, Sinai Hospital, and a dense network of federal agencies in the metro add employment depth that insulates Baltimore’s rental market from single-employer risk.
The unique Baltimore insight: the city’s 19th-century row house stock creates an investor efficiency that newer-construction markets cannot replicate. A Baltimore row house delivers 1,400 to 1,800 square feet on a narrow brick footprint, requires less maintenance per unit than scattered single-family properties, and produces DSCR ratios that comparable investment in suburban Maryland cannot achieve at similar acquisition prices.
Fells Point and the Inner Harbor area drive STR demand through Ravens and Orioles game traffic, the National Aquarium, and a packed waterfront events calendar. Weekend STR rates in Fells Point and Canton can reach $150 to $220 per night during home game weekends — income projections that push DSCR ratios above 1.25 even on higher-acquisition-price waterfront properties.

Best Investment Areas in Baltimore
Charles Village & Remington — Hopkins Corridor Core
Charles Village sits directly north of Johns Hopkins University’s Homewood Campus. Remington borders it to the west and has been the focus of a decade of investment driven by Hopkins proximity and the mixed-use Stacks development. Medical residents, researchers, and university staff rent consistently in both neighborhoods.
Row houses: $200,000–$380,000. Monthly 2–3BR rents: $1,400–$2,100. DSCR: 1.15–1.35. Institutional renter demand drives structurally low vacancy.
Hampden — ‘The Avenue’ Walkability Premium
Hampden’s 36th Street commercial corridor — known locally as ‘The Avenue’ — gives the neighborhood a retail and dining identity that drives persistent renter demand from creatives, young professionals, and Hopkins-adjacent renters who want neighborhood character without Hopkins pricing pressure.
Row houses: $195,000–$350,000. 2BR rents: $1,300–$1,800/month. DSCR: 1.15–1.30. Consistent appreciation alongside reliable cash flow.
Fells Point & Canton — Waterfront STR and Premium Long-Term
Fells Point’s cobblestone streets, bar scene, and waterfront access drive weekend tourism that supports real STR income. Canton’s harbor views and restaurant corridor attract professional renters paying top-of-market rates for Baltimore’s most walkable waterfront living.
Row houses and condos: $280,000–$550,000. Long-term 2BR rents: $1,600–$2,400. STR nightly during Ravens/Orioles weekends: $150–$220. Projected STR DSCR frequently exceeds 1.25.
South Baltimore & Pigtown — BRRRR and Value Cash Flow
South Baltimore and Pigtown (Washington Village) sit southwest of M&T Bank Stadium within walking distance of the Inner Harbor. Acquisition prices are Baltimore’s most accessible for value-add investors. BRRRR activity has been steady here for years.
Row houses in various condition: $85,000–$175,000. Post-rehab 3BR rents: $1,150–$1,500. Stabilized DSCR: 1.30–1.55. Strong cash-on-cash for investors with rehab capability.
Belair-Edison & Northeast Baltimore — Portfolio Cash Flow Zone
Belair-Edison and adjacent Northeast Baltimore neighborhoods offer accessible pricing with consistent working-class and service-sector renter demand. These are stable, long-established rental communities — not transitional zones — with vacancy patterns that DSCR lenders find straightforward to underwrite.
Row houses: $90,000–$155,000. Monthly rents: $1,050–$1,400. DSCR: 1.25–1.55. Best gross yield ratios in Baltimore at the most accessible acquisition prices.
Towson & Baltimore County Suburbs — Institutional Suburban Demand
Towson is Baltimore County’s commercial hub with Towson University (23,000 students) and proximity to Johns Hopkins Bayview Medical Campus. Student and healthcare professional renter demand is persistent, predictable, and easier to manage than inner-city properties.
Townhomes and SFR: $230,000–$390,000. 3BR rents: $1,600–$2,200. DSCR: 1.10–1.25. Lower management intensity with stable institutional renter profiles.
