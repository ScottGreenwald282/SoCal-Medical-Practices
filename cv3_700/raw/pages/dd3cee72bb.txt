What Is a DSCR Loan and Why Does It Matter in NJ? A DSCR loan — Debt Service Coverage Ratio loan — qualifies borrowers based on a property&#x27;s rental income rather than the investor&#x27;s personal income. The formula is simple: monthly gross rent divided by monthly PITIA (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, association dues). A ratio of 1.0 means the property breaks even on debt service. A ratio above 1.0 means positive cash flow. For New Jersey investors, DSCR loans solve a specific problem that conventional financing creates. NJ property taxes — the highest in the nation at 2.0–2.5% of assessed value — mean that the PITIA payment on any NJ property is significantly higher than an equivalent property in a lower-tax state. Conventional lenders using W2-based underwriting often can&#x27;t approve NJ investors because their personal income appears insufficient relative to their debt obligations. DSCR loans bypass this entirely by focusing on whether the rent covers the payment — which, in strong NJ rental markets, it often does. New Jersey DSCR Loan Requirements in 2026 Requirements vary by lender and program, but standard benchmarks across most NJ DSCR programs in 2026 include a down payment of 15 % or more on purchases, a DSCR ratio of 1.0 or above, and 3–6 months of PITIA in liquid reserves post-closing. Loan amounts range from $75,000 to $25 million depending on property type. No tax returns, W2s, pay stubs, or employment verification are required. Personal debt-to-income ratio is not calculated. You can hold the property in an LLC, and there is no limit on the number of DSCR-financed properties you can own. The NJ Property Tax Problem — and How to Solve It The single biggest challenge for NJ DSCR deals is the tax component of PITIA. A $400,000 property in New Jersey carries roughly $8,000–$10,000 in annual property taxes — $667–$833 per month. That amount gets added directly to your mortgage payment for DSCR calculation purposes. This can push a deal that would easily qualify in another state below the 1.0 DSCR threshold. There are three strategies that work in NJ&#x27;s high-tax environment. First, target markets with better rent-to-price ratios — Southern NJ markets like Cherry Hill and Atlantic County offer lower purchase prices relative to rents, producing more favorable DSCR math. Second, increase your down payment — reducing the loan balance reduces the P&amp;I component and improves the DSCR ratio. Third, focus on multi-unit properties — the combined rent from a 2–4 unit property creates more income to cover the elevated PITIA, often producing stronger DSCR ratios than single-family in the same municipality.
