Check out 20 Investor Questions and The Ultimate Guide to DSCR Mortgage Loans in Texas for Real Estate Investors . What is a DSCR mortgage loan? DSCR measures whether the rental income from the property is enough to cover the property’s monthly housing payment. Debt service = the mortgage payment and sometimes other housing costs Coverage ratio = how much rent covers the monthly payment A basic DSCR formula looks like this: DSCR = Monthly rental income ÷ Monthly housing payment Example: Rent = $2,200/month Monthly housing payment = $2,000/month DSCR = 2,200 ÷ 2,000 = 1.10 A DSCR of 1.00 means the rent equals the payment. Above 1.00 means rent covers the payment with room to spare. Below 1.00 means rent does not fully cover the payment. Different lenders set different minimum DSCR requirements, and those requirements can affect your rate, down payment, and approval strength. Why DSCR loans are so popular in Texas Texas is a huge investor market—rental demand, population growth, and job movement have made single-family rentals and small multifamily properties attractive in many areas. DSCR loans are popular because they can: Avoid heavy income documentation (no tax returns required) Qualify you based on the property’s cash flow Work well for investors building a portfolio (multiple properties) Move faster than many traditional “full-doc” investor loans If your personal income is complex (business write-offs, fluctuating commissions, multiple streams), DSCR is often a cleaner approach. Who is a DSCR loan best for? A DSCR loan is typically a great fit if you are: Buying a rental property (long-term rental) Buying a property to operate as a short-term rental (depending on program rules) Refinancing a rental property (rate/term or cash-out, depending on guidelines) Self-employed and don’t want your write-offs to hurt your qualifying income A real estate investor scaling beyond what conventional loans easily allow It’s less ideal if you’re buying a primary residence (DSCR is usually for investment properties only) or if the rent won’t support the payment and the lender requires a higher DSCR. What types of properties can qualify Most DSCR programs are built for typical investment property types, such as: Single-family homes 2–4 unit properties Condos (program-dependent) Townhomes How rental income is calculated (and why it matters) For most DSCR loans, rental income is supported by one of these: Appraisal with a market rent analysis ( called a rent schedule) Existing lease agreement (if already leased) For short-term rentals, some programs may allow alternative documentation (varies widely) The key point: the lender will use a recognized method to estimate rent. If the rent supports the payment strongly, your terms generally improve. What counts in the monthly “housing payment” This is a big deal because your DSCR depends on what the lender includes. Often it’s based on: Principal + Interest Property taxes Insurance HOA dues Typical DSCR thresholds (what lenders like to see) Common DSCR ranges you’ll hear: 1.00+ : rent covers the payment (often a key target) 1.10–1.25 : stronger approvals and better pricing Below 1.00 : still possible sometimes, but terms may be tighter (bigger down payment, higher reserves, or higher rate) &nbsp; Down payment and equity expectations DSCR loans are investment loans, so they usually require more skin in the game than a primary home loan. You’ll commonly see: Purchases: often 20%–25% down (sometimes more depending on DSCR, credit, and property type) Refinances: based on maximum loan-to-value (LTV), which varies by program Higher down payment (or more equity) can: Improve pricing Improve approval odds Offset a weaker DSCR Credit score and borrower requirements DSCR programs usually have credit score minimums, and stronger credit tends to help pricing. Lenders may also look at: Major credit events and how recent they are Real estate experience (sometimes) Cash reserves (more on this next) The DSCR loan is “property income focused,” but it’s still a mortgage—credit and capacity still matter. Cash reserves Many DSCR lenders require reserves, meaning money you have available after closing on a purchase or refinance loan. This could be: A certain number of months of mortgage payments in the bank Liquid assets or sometimes retirement accounts Reserves can increase based on: Number of financed properties Property type Loan size Texas Premier Mortgage will match you with the best program and reserve requirements. DSCR purchase vs DSCR refinance (including cash-out) DSCR works for both buying and refinancing. DSCR Purchase Good for acquiring new rental properties without traditional income qualification. DSCR Rate-and-Term Refinance Used to lower rate, change terms, or remove someone from title (scenario dependent). DSCR Cash-Out Refinance This is popular for investors pulling equity to: Buy another property Renovate Pay off higher-interest debt tied to the property Build reserves What documents you’ll typically need Compared to traditional loans, DSCR is often lighter on income documentation, but you still provide standard mortgage paperwork. Common items include: ID and basic borrower info Property contract (for purchase) Insurance quote Appraisal (ordered through the lender) Lease agreement (if applicable) Bank statements / asset proof for down payment and reserves Many DSCR programs don’t require W-2s or tax returns in the traditional way, but requirements can vary.
