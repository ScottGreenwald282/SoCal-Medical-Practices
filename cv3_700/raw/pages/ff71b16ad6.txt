DSCR Mortgage Loan FAQ
What does DSCR stand for?
DSCR stands for Debt Service Coverage Ratio. It measures whether a rental property’s income can cover its mortgage payment.
How is DSCR calculated?
DSCR is calculated by dividing the property’s monthly rental income by the total monthly mortgage payment. A higher ratio indicates stronger cash flow.
DSCR = Monthly rental income ÷ Monthly housing payment
Example:
Rent = $2,200/month
Monthly housing payment = $2,000/month
DSCR = 2,200 ÷ 2,000 =
1.10
A DSCR of
1.00
means the rent equals the payment.
Above
1.00
means rent covers the payment with room to spare.
Below
1.00
means rent does not fully cover the payment.
Different lenders set different minimum DSCR requirements, and those requirements can affect your rate, down payment, and approval strength.
Do DSCR loans require income verification?
No. DSCR loans typically do not require tax returns, W-2s, or pay stubs. Approval is based on property income.
Can I use a DSCR loan for multiple properties?
Yes. Many investors use DSCR loans to finance multiple rental properties because each property qualifies based on its own income.
What credit score is needed for a DSCR loan?
Credit score requirements vary by lender, but many DSCR programs start around the 660 range, with rates dramatically increasing for lower credit scores. Higher down payments can help offset lower credit scores to keep the rates down and help the qualification process.
Are DSCR loans only for Texas?
DSCR Loans
are available nationwide, but Steve Head specializes in DSCR mortgage loans for Texas real estate investors.
Can DSCR loans be used for cash-out refinancing?
Yes. DSCR loans can be used for cash-out refinances, allowing investors to access equity from rental properties.
Are DSCR loans good for first-time investors?
Yes. DSCR loans can be a strong option for first-time investors who have a qualifying rental property and want to avoid traditional income documentation.
