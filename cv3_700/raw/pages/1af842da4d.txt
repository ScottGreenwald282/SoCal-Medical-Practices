We got you covered, let’s discuss it Get started How We Evaluate DSCR Loans DSCR stands for Debt Service Coverage Ratio. We use it to compare the monthly rental income of a property to its monthly mortgage payment. That payment usually includes principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and HOA fees when applicable. If a property rents for $2,800 a month and the total monthly payment is $2,400, the DSCR is 1.17. All lenders require a minimum DSCR of 1.0, which means the rent must cover the full payment. Even if your result is below 1.0, you can still qualify — but the lender will require a larger down payment of around 25%. The rental income figure comes from the appraisal. The appraiser puts together a rent schedule based on what comparable units are renting for in that area. We review that number carefully and make sure your file is structured correctly before it goes to the lender. Looking for a mortgage? What You Need to Qualify One thing to address upfront: reserves. After your down payment and closing costs are covered, most lenders still want to see 3 to 6 months of mortgage payments sitting in a verifiable account. That catches people off guard sometimes, especially when they've already set aside the down payment and assumed they were done. At LBC Mortgage, we flag this early so there are no surprises late in the process. On the credit side, most DSCR programs start at a 620 FICO score . You don't need perfect credit, but the lower your score, the higher your rate will be. That's just how non-QM pricing works — the rate compensates for the risk. Down payment starts at 15 percent for most borrowers. If the property's DSCR comes in below 1.0 — meaning the rent doesn't fully cover the payment — the lender will typically require around 25 percent down instead. Rates and Prepayment California DSCR loan rates are slightly higher than conventional investment property rates. That is the trade-off for not having to document personal income, and it gives investors the opportunity to qualify for properties they otherwise couldn't finance. Most DSCR loans carry a prepayment penalty, but borrowers have the flexibility to choose from zero to five years of prepayment. If you plan to hold the property short-term, a one- to three-year prepayment option is a natural fit. If you plan to keep the property long-term, accepting a prepayment penalty can actually work in your favor — it lowers your rate . And if you plan to sell or simply don't want any prepayment restriction, you can choose that option as well. What DSCR mortgage loan options does LBC Mortgage offer for investment home buyers in California? DSCR mortgage loans in California are structured for investment home buyers who want to finance income-producing residential properties without relying on personal employment income. LBC Mortgage provides DSCR loan options that evaluate a property’s projected or current rental income in relation to its monthly debt obligations. Instead of calculating traditional debt-to-income ratios based on W-2 wages or tax returns, lenders assess whether the rental income sufficiently covers the mortgage payment, including principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and applicable association dues. Loan terms commonly include 30-year fixed-rate options, adjustable-rate structures, and in some cases interest-only periods designed to improve short-term cash flow. Eligible property types generally include single-family rentals , townhomes, condominiums, and small multifamily properties. Minimum DSCR thresholds often begin around 0.75, with improved pricing and lower risk tiers available at higher coverage levels. These programs are widely used for both property acquisition and refinance transactions, allowing investors to scale portfolios or restructure debt based on asset performance rather than personal income limitations. 2. How do DSCR mortgage loans work for California investment home buyers? A DSCR mortgage loan works by analyzing the relationship between a property’s rental income and its total monthly debt obligations. For investment home buyers in California, the lender calculates the Debt Service Coverage Ratio by dividing the property’s monthly rental income by the projected monthly mortgage payment. A ratio of 1.0 indicates that rental income exactly covers the debt payment, while higher ratios reflect stronger cash flow. Some programs allow ratios below 1.0, typically requiring higher down payments or stronger credit profiles to offset reduced coverage. Because qualification focuses on the property rather than the borrower’s personal income, DSCR loans eliminate the need for W-2 forms, paystubs, or traditional tax return analysis. Appraisals commonly include market rent schedules to validate projected income. This approach enables investors to grow portfolios without being restricted by personal debt-to-income limits. In California’s competitive rental markets, DSCR loans align financing decisions with the economic performance of the asset itself. 3. What down payment is required for a DSCR mortgage loan? Down payment requirements for DSCR mortgage loans in California typically begin at approximately 20% of the property’s purchase price. However, the required equity contribution may increase to 25% or more depending on the property’s rental income strength, the borrower’s credit profile, and overall risk assessment. Properties with lower DSCR ratios often require higher down payments to offset potential cash-flow volatility. In addition to the down payment, lenders usually require post-closing reserves equal to several months of total housing payments to ensure liquidity during vacancy or market fluctuations. These reserve requirements are particularly important in rental property financing, where tenant turnover can temporarily affect income stability. The purpose of these structured equity standards is to maintain sustainable leverage while still offering flexible qualification based on rental performance. For investment home buyers, understanding loan-to-value limits and reserve expectations is essential when planning acquisitions in California’s high-demand housing markets. 4. What property types qualify for DSCR loans? DSCR mortgage loans in California are intended for residential properties that generate or are expected to generate rental income. Eligible property types commonly include single-family homes used as rentals, townhomes, and warrantable condominiums. Many programs also support small multifamily properties, typically up to four units, provided they meet underwriting and appraisal standards. In certain cases, properties with mixed-use characteristics may qualify if residential rental income represents the primary revenue source. Short-term rental eligibility varies by city and lender guidelines, particularly in areas with restrictive zoning or licensing requirements. During underwriting, lenders assess property condition, occupancy status, and market rental comparables to confirm income projections. Appraisal reports often include a rent schedule that supports DSCR calculations. This flexible property eligibility allows investment home buyers to diversify portfolios across different residential asset classes while maintaining a consistent financing framework based on rental performance in California markets. 5. When should an investment home buyer choose a DSCR mortgage instead of a conventional investor loan in California? An investment home buyer may select a DSCR mortgage instead of a conventional investor loan when personal debt-to-income ratios or tax return reporting limit borrowing capacity. Conventional investor loans require full income verification and apply strict personal income ratios that can restrict portfolio expansion. DSCR loans remove this constraint by focusing primarily on the property’s income performance rather than the borrower’s employment or tax documentation. This structure is especially useful for investors who hold multiple properties, claim significant depreciation, or operate through business entities. While DSCR loans may carry slightly higher interest rates or require larger down payments compared to conventional loans, the tradeoff is greater flexibility and scalability. Borrowers should evaluate projected rental income, long-term hold strategy, and exit planning when determining whether DSCR financing aligns with their objectives. In California’s high-value rental markets, DSCR loans serve as a strategic tool for expanding investment portfolios without being constrained by personal income limits.
